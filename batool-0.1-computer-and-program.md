# Phase 0 · Session 0.1 — What Is a Computer? What Is a Program?
**Batool — Private Coding Mentorship — Month 1**
**Duration:** 90 min · **Format:** Mostly unplugged + light diagram support
**Language for this pack:** English (translate live in-session per course language policy — Arabic carries explanation, English is reserved for the bolded terms)

## Session goal
Before writing any code, Batool can explain — in her own words — what a computer is and what
a program is, using the input → process → output model. No syntax, no Scratch today.

## Why this session exists
This is the foundational layer the syntax-first plan was missing: every later concept
(variable, loop, function) needs a mental model to land on. Skipping this risks her
memorizing syntax without understanding what's actually happening underneath.

## PRIMM breakdown

### Predict (10 min)
Ask her: "If I asked you to make tea, could you do it without step-by-step instructions?
Why?" Let her articulate that humans fill gaps with common sense. Then: "If you told a
computer 'make tea,' would it know how?" Capture her prediction without correcting it yet.

### Run (15 min — unplugged "be the computer" activity)
Give her two versions of the same recipe: one with a deliberately vague step ("add a good
amount of sugar"), one fully precise. Have her (or you, playing "the computer") follow each
*literally*, doing nothing except exactly what's written. The vague one breaks or produces
something silly; the precise one works every time. This physical experience IS the lesson,
before it's named.

### Investigate (20 min)
Introduce vocabulary mapped onto what she just did:
- **Program** — the written list of exact steps.
- **Instruction** — one single step in that list.
- **Execute / Run** — a computer (or a person acting like one) following the steps, one at a
  time, in order, with zero interpretation.
- **Input** — what you give it to start.
- **Process** — what happens while it's working.
- **Output** — what comes out at the end.

Draw the **Input → Process → Output** diagram together — she draws it herself. This is the
single most reusable model in the whole course.

Then, briefly and concretely (using her own phone/laptop):
- **CPU** — the part that actually does the steps, the "worker."
- **Memory / RAM** — a desk where the worker keeps what it's using *right now* — cleared
  when the desk is cleared (device turned off).
- **Storage** — a filing cabinet — keeps things even after the desk is cleared.

Keep this light — one clear analogy each, no depth beyond it today.

### Modify (25 min)
Give her 3 more everyday instruction sets written with vague steps (brushing teeth, tying a
shoelace, walking from her room to the kitchen). 
Her job: rewrite each so a literal-minded
"computer" could follow it without breaking.

### Make (15 min)
She writes one brand-new instruction set from scratch for something she picks herself (making
a sandwich, packing a school bag) — precise enough for a computer-brain to follow. She then
labels which parts are input, process, and output.

### Wrap-up (5 min)
Have her explain to an imaginary younger sibling: "What is a computer, and what is a
program?" This conversation is the real check, not a quiz.

## What "done" looks like
Unprompted, she can:
- Explain a computer does exactly what it's told, not what you meant.
- Use program / instruction / execute / input / process / output correctly.
- Point at input/process/output in code-free content.

If not yet solid, re-run Investigate/Modify next time before 0.2 — per principle #1.

# Practice Sheet — What Is a Computer? What Is a Program?

## 🟢 Warm-Up
1. In your own words: does a computer do exactly what you tell it, or what you mean? Why?
2. Match each term to its meaning:

   | Term | Meaning |
   |---|---|
   | Program | ? |
   | Instruction | ? |
   | Execute | ? |
   | Input | ? |
   | Output | ? |

## 🟡 Practice
For each instruction, decide: is it **precise** (a computer could follow it with no
confusion) or **vague** (needs clarifying)?
1. "Add some sugar to the tea."
2. "Press the red button once."
3. "Walk until you arrive."
4. "Open the door, enter the room, close the door behind you."

If vague, rewrite it precisely.

## 🔴 Challenge
Pick a simple daily activity (making a sandwich, packing your school bag) and write a
**very precise** instruction list — precise enough that someone with zero context could
follow it exactly. Then label your own list:
- 🔵 the **Input** part
- 🟣 the **Process** part
- 🟢 the **Output** part

## 💭 Reflection
- What surprised you most today about how a computer "thinks"?
- Was there a moment your instruction wasn't precise enough? What was missing?
